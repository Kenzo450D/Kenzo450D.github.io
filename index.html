Introduction

My journey in robotics is guided by a simple realization: for a robot to achieve high-level autonomy, it must tightly couple its perception systems with its planning and navigation loops. Having researched and worked across both disciplines, I focus on building robust, real-world autonomy stacks.

I graduated with my Ph.D. in Computer Science in May 2024 from UNC Charlotte, under the guidance of Dr. Srinivas Akella. I advanced the field of autonomous exploration during my doctoral research by developing highly optimized time-sensitive planning algorithms for strict deadline constraints.

Prior to my Ph.D., I laid my foundations in robot perception as an M.S. student at the Robotics Research Center (RRC) at IIIT Hyderabad, working on multi-scale SLAM pose-graph robustification and topological graph analysis.

Current Focus

I currently work on planning and navigation systems for warehouse robotics. In warehouse environments, robots must solve high-velocity, high-density planning problems while operating alongside dynamic obstacles and uncertainty. My goal is to build planning architectures that seamlessly integrate live perception data for optimal, safety-certified behavior.

I am ever-curious and actively looking for opportunities in perception and planning roles where I can apply my combined background in graph SLAM, autonomous exploration, and multi-robot trajectory planning to solve complex industry challenges.
